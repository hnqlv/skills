# Forbidden Patterns

Remove these patterns before returning prose. Preserve a real contrast or reference only when accuracy requires it, and rewrite it directly.

- **Staccato pairs:** two clipped fragments or sentences used to manufacture rhythm. Combine them into one natural sentence or state the stronger point once.
- **Antithesis reframe / negative parallelism:** constructions such as `It is not X. It is Y.` that manufacture contrast. State Y directly and include X only when the distinction changes the meaning.
- **Isocolon metaphor-pairs:** balanced clauses or matched metaphors used for polish rather than precision. Replace them with the concrete relationship or action.
- **Backward references:** phrases such as `this`, `that`, `the former`, or `as noted above` without an immediate, unmistakable referent. Name the subject or restate the needed fact.
- **Inflated phrases:** long constructions that have a direct equivalent, such as `due to the fact that` or `lacked the ability to`. Use `because`, `could not`, or another plain equivalent.
- **Unsupported qualifiers and weasel words:** adjectives, adverbs, buzzwords, or phrases such as `significantly better`, `nearly all`, and `much faster` without evidence. Use available data or an observable result; never invent a metric.
- **Mechanical presentation defaults:** repeated em dashes, bold lead-ins, rhetorical questions, or one-line paragraphs used as automatic rhythm. Keep them only when they perform a clear structural job.

Add each newly observed recurring pattern as one bullet with its cue and direct replacement rule. Keep the list specific enough to enforce mechanically.
