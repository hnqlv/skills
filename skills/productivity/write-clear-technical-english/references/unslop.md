# Unslop

Adapted from [Cursor's Unslop](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/blob/e8d856f0273b42ebafe0ec3546bd645709e7c1b0/pstack/skills/unslop/SKILL.md), revision `e8d856f0273b42ebafe0ec3546bd645709e7c1b0`. Copyright (c) 2026 Lauren Tan. See [MIT license](unslop-LICENSE.txt).

Scan the draft, rewrite the matching patterns, then check once for remaining filler or awkward phrasing. Preserve meaning and the intended tone.

## Remove or rewrite

- Empty claims such as "highlighting the importance" or "ensuring excellence." State the mechanism or result, or delete the claim.
- Vague attribution such as "experts believe." Name the source or remove the attribution.
- Inflated vocabulary: use "use" for "utilize," "help" for "facilitate," and "is" for "serves as."
- Invented jargon and abstract metaphors. Name the actual component, operation, or constraint. Keep established domain terms when they are precise.
- Manufactured contrasts such as "not just X, but Y," matched slogans, and forced groups of three. State the point directly.
- Synonym cycling and vague references. Use one term per concept and make each pronoun's subject clear.
- False ranges such as "from reliability to collaboration." Name the actual topics.
- Decorative dashes, mid-sentence colons, bold labels that repeat the sentence, emojis, and title-case headings. Use ordinary sentences and formatting that helps scanning.
- Chatbot greetings, flattery, generic conclusions, and invitations to continue that add no information.
- Filler such as "in order to" and "it is important to note." Use "to" or delete it.
- Stacked hedges, unsupported qualifiers, and adverbs propping up weak verbs. Preserve real uncertainty; use available evidence instead of invented precision.
- Claims about how code feels. Say what it does and what the reader can observe.
- Dense sentences that require rereading. Split separate ideas; keep a condition beside its action.
- Passive constructions that obscure responsibility. Name the actor when it matters.
- Mannered fragments, personified code, and compressed symbol-speak. Use complete sentences with articles and verbs.

## Final check

Can the reader identify what happened, why it matters, and any required action without decoding the prose? Cut repetition and decoration, then check that the remaining claims still match the evidence.

This adaptation condenses the upstream catalog, retains local guidance on ambiguous references, and treats punctuation as an editorial choice. Exact quotations, technical syntax, and requested formats take precedence.
