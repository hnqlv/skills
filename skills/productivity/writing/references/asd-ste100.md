# ASD-STE100 technical English

Authority: [ASD-STE100, Issue 9, 2025-01-15](https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf). [Official access page](https://asd-ste100.org/STE_downloads.html).

## Working checklist

- Verify Part 2 vocabulary: meaning, part of speech, and inflection. Technical nouns and verbs must fit permitted categories. Keep terminology consistent (section 1).
- Limit noun groups to three words, preserving longer official technical names before defining shorter forms (section 2).
- Use simple tenses and approved forms. Avoid complex auxiliaries. Past participles serve as adjectives; “-ing” belongs only within technical nouns. Use active voice; descriptive passive requires an unknown actor (section 3).
- Retain necessary sentence elements, including articles. Avoid contractions (section 4).
- Procedures: at most 20 words per sentence. Use commands, one instruction per sentence except simultaneous actions, and prerequisite conditions first. Notes contain information only (section 5).
- Descriptions: at most 25 words per sentence; one topic and at most six sentences per paragraph (section 6).
- Safety text: identify severity, command or condition, and risk (section 7).
- Avoid semicolons. Apply STE word-count conventions for lists, parentheses, identifiers, and hyphens (section 8).
- Reconstruct sentences when substitution fails. Avoid phrasal verbs (section 9).

## Use the standard

This checklist is a navigation aid, not the complete standard or dictionary. Consult the relevant rules and Part 2 entries when writing technical English. Retrieve the official PDF or use an available authorized copy; do not vendor the specification into this skill.

For a compliance review, check both parts and record any unresolved terms or required-text exceptions. If the standard is unavailable, continue with this checklist and disclose that full STE verification was not possible when relevant. Never label text fully compliant from this checklist alone or assume that familiar software jargon is approved vocabulary.
